Runway, step 1, third draft — built on your suggestion: two shapes are the same when their records are indistinguishable.

Three shapes, one set of pushes

Below are three shapes that look nothing alike: a flat band whose two edges count as the same spot, a square loop, and a lumpy loop. A hopper stands on each. One push moves all three hoppers the same fraction of the way around their own shape. That fraction is the only thing you control.

One record

Each push adds one column to the track. The mark is how far around the hopper stood — as a fraction of its shape. All three shapes write the same column every time, so there is one track, not three.

Which shape made this track?

There is no answer. The track is identical for all three. Anything the record cannot tell apart, the rule does not care about: the band, the square and the lump are the same shape as far as returning goes. Roundness, corners, lumps, a top and a bottom — none of it reaches the record. That is what "a circle" means here, and it is all it means: whatever leaves this kind of track.

This is why the round picture in the first draft was misleading: it was one of these three, pretending to be the only one.

Repeat or never

With 1/4 the track repeats every 4 columns; with 3/7 every 7. With the golden number — 0.618033…, not a fraction of whole numbers — it never repeats; new marks keep landing in the spaces between old ones. Stop it anywhere and the spaces between marks come in at most three sizes (Steinhaus, 1950s); the readout under the band counts them.

The one question

Someone tells you: "I pushed the golden hopper 100 times and it stood exactly on its starting spot." What would you check before believing it?

Push 100 times and measure. It ends of the way around from the start — near, never zero. A fraction of whole numbers returns exactly; the golden number only comes near. Exact return versus near return is step 1.

The first shape the track can tell apart

Glue the band's edges with a flip — the top of the right edge meets the bottom of the left. Now the hopper comes back upside-down, and the record changes: it takes two trips round to be back exactly as you started. The track can see that. That is step 2, and it's the first time a shape earns a name of its own.
