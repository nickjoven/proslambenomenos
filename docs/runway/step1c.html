Axes Are Moves

Runway, step 1c. You have x and y: "step sideways" and "step forward." Here are four more axes, each added the same way — as a new kind of move — and each one is also a new way of "do this and nothing changes."

The pattern

Every axis here was added by adding a kind of move, and every kind of move brought a symmetry: slide (x, y), turn (heading), zoom (scale), mirror (flip). And step 1's axis was push (time). "Axis of symmetry" is not a line drawn on a picture — it is a move you are allowed to make without anything changing.

Panel 2 is the important warning. The rule was fair and the reach still wasn't round, because one of the axes was hidden from the picture. When a shadow looks lopsided, the first question is not "what's wrong with the rule" but "which axis am I not seeing?"

The one question

In panel 2 the car's reach leans forward. Someone says: "so forward is a special direction — the rule isn't fair." What would you check?

Start the car facing a different way. The page re-runs panel 2 with every starting heading and overlays them: . The union is round. The rule never preferred forward; the start did — and "start facing east" is a choice on the hidden heading axis, not a fact about the moves. Fairness lives on all the axes at once, including the ones the picture doesn't show.
