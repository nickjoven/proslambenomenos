The Butterfly Plane

Harper's equation at λ = 1: for each rational flux α = p/q the spectrum splits into q bands (vertical dark segments), and every gap between them carries two integers, computed live wherever you point:

r = s·q + t·p,  |t| ≤ q/2  —  unique; t is the Chern number (Streda: t = dN/dα)

Topology is the coloring: gaps are tinted by t (warm for t > 0, cool for t < 0, brighter = smaller |t|), and a clicked gap's family line is traced across every flux that carries the same (s, t) — the gap as one object living over the whole plane. Algebra is the HUD: the Diophantine solution for the exact gap under your cursor. Press T for the Wannier view (α vs integrated density of states N = r/q), where every gap family becomes a straight line N = s + tα whose slope is the Chern number.

Zoom in: the lattice of rationals refines as you go (q grows with magnification), so the butterfly's self-similarity is something you navigate rather than admire. The dashed gold line is the golden mean 1/φ; along its Fibonacci convergents the P-28 map holds: the gap at r = Fj carries |t| = Fn−j.
